## Spec tripwire disposition

The brief's tripwire — "if the candidate median is slower than baseline
AT ALL, investigate before proceeding" — did **not** fire: every one of
the 6 works, and the workset median, show the candidate strictly faster
than baseline. No repeat session was needed. This matches the expected
direction: Phase 2 removed the subprocess hop (baseline is
`aozora-adapter` shelling out to the pinned upstream `aozora` binary via
`AB_AOZORA_BIN`; candidate is the native in-process `ab-aozora` AAT
producer with no child process at all), so end-to-end wall time should
drop, and it does substantially.

## Non-comparability note (important)

**This report is not comparable to Phase 1's perf gate**
(`docs/superpowers/reports/2026-07-10-fork-parity-perf.md`,
`2026-07-10-fork-parity-perf.json`). Phase 1 measured the candidate's
*inner* binary (`ab-aozora-cli inspect nodes -`, no adapter, no AAT
serialization) against upstream `aozora inspect nodes -` directly —
same lifted code, thin CLI shim only, workset median ~0.215s both lanes,
essentially a no-op comparison (-0.07%). Phase 2 instead measures the
full **end-to-end AAT-production path**: baseline is the adapter process
tree (`aozora-adapter --mode aat` shelling out to pinned upstream
`aozora`), candidate is the new native `ab-aozora --mode aat` binary that
absorbed AAT production directly (no subprocess, no adapter framing on
the candidate side). The ~5x larger baseline medians here (0.3-0.9s vs
Phase 1's 0.12-0.37s) reflect the adapter's subprocess-spawn and AAT
JSON-encoding overhead added on top of the same inner parse — not a
regression in the parser itself. Do not diff these numbers against
Phase 1's; they measure different call surfaces under a different mode.
