# Ortho-Detect Phase 2.5 — 300-Sentence LLM-Labeled Evaluation Set Measurement

Date: 2026-07-05
Gold set: `data/ortho-gold/sentences-llm-300.jsonl` (n=300, LLM-labeled)
Sampler: `scripts/ortho-gold/sample_300.py` (corpus-representative, Tanizaki 0.65)
Labeler: orchestrator LLM applying the spec's intent rubric (NOT a human
Japanese-lit expert — treat as second-tier bootstrap).

## Bias caveats (read first)

1. **LLM labeler, not human:** the labels are an LLM's application of the
   rubric. 10% spot-check (30 records) with a reframed rubric gave agreement
   0.933 (above the 0.80 gate). Two first-pass errors found and corrected
   (idx 100, 150, both grammatical prose mislabeled reject). Still NOT ground
   truth — a human Japanese-lit expert would catch more.
2. **ML recall = train-accuracy (upper bound):** the ML detector was trained
   on these 300 records and is evaluated on them. This is a CEILING, not
   generalization. HeuristicV1 recall is real generalization (no train/eval
   split). Hold-out CV is Task 6 (conditional on the branch decision).
3. **N=300, not 500:** the spec's target range is 200-500. 300 is within
   range. A final human-expert pass before any production promotion is the
   documented finishing input.


- **Recall: 0.8263** — below the phase's 0.85 target. The single number the
  branch decision turns on; the LLM label ranks HeuristicV1 under threshold.
- **Precision: 0.9514** — strong. When HeuristicV1 fires, it is almost
  always right (9 false positives out of 185 predicted-accept).
- **F1: 0.8844.**
- **Disagreements: 46** (37 FN + 9 FP), i.e. 15.3% of the set vs bootstrap.

### False-negative characterization

The 37 FNs are overwhelmingly the **prose-at-low-ratio** sentences the
Phase 2 probe predicted. Plotting their character-level katakana ratio:

| ratio bucket | count | interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| 0.43–0.55 | 22 | mixed prose: katakana words/morphemes inline in hiragana prose |
| 0.55–0.65 | 4 | transitional — borderline ratio, label == accept |
| 0.65–0.80 | 11 | higher-ratio prose still mis-rejected |

Sample FNs illustrate the shape — they are grammatical prose sentences
containing clitics/particles in katakana (`ソレカラ`, `僕ハ`, `風呂場`, etc.)
rather than the pure-katakana block the heuristic was tuned for:

- `ソノ時僕ハ第四次元ノ世界ニ突入シタトイウ気ガシタ。` (ratio ≈ 0.46)
- `カエッテ僕ヤ木村ノ方ガ紅イ顔ニナッタ。` (ratio ≈ 0.63)
- `今夜モ妻ハ中座シテ便所ニ隠レ、ソレカラ風呂場（風呂ハ…` (ratio ≈ 0.47)
- `郁子ヨ、ワガ愛スルイトシノ妻ヨ、…` (ratio ≈ 0.65)

This matches the Phase 2 probe's hypothesis: HeuristicV1's ratio/length
filter is calibrated for the high-ratio-block case and systematically
under-rejects katakana-in-running-prose. A non-trivial share (~30%) of
the FNs sit at high ratio (0.65–0.80) and look like they should already
pass — these point at either (a) the proper-noun guard mis-firing on
katakana prose, or (b) a length/threshold interaction worth a closer
look in Phase 3.

### False-positive characterization (9)

The 9 FPs are the inverse failure: short, very-high-ratio lines the LLM
labeled `reject` (onomatopoeia, standalone foreign proper nouns, title
fragments) that HeuristicV1 accepts on ratio alone:

- `チャチャラカ、チャカポコ。` (onomatopoeia — not "orthographic" >
  not an accept target)
- `ドストイエフスキイ。` (standalone proper noun)
- `ヴォラプチュアス！`

These are labeling-rubric edge cases (is "an all-katakana interjection"
an orthographic-katakana-to-hiragana target?). They are not a precision
problem in the detector's design sense; they reflect where the rubric's
intent boundary falls. A human-expert pass should resolve them.

## Branch decision (Task 4)

**Decision rule application** (per `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-07-05-ortho-detect-phase2.5.md` Task 4):

- HeuristicV1 recall = 0.8263 < 0.85 → the spec's recall floor is violated. A fix is REQUIRED (not optional). Task 5 (threshold sweep + tune) fires.
- ML ceiling = 0.9577, recall gap over HeuristicV1 = +0.1315 (≥ 0.10) AND ≥ 0.95 AND HeuristicV1 < 0.85 → the third branch fires: **execute Task 5 (heuristic threshold tune as the immediate fix to clear the floor), Task 6 (ML k-fold hold-out CV to validate ML generalization), and Task 7 (promote `--ortho-detect ml` from EXPERIMENTAL to stable, NOT default) ONLY if Task 6 mean recall ≥ 0.85.**
- **Task 8 (code deletion) does NOT fire** — it is deferred to a future phase with real human annotation per the Phase 2 ADR. The LLM-labeled evaluation set is NOT human ground truth and cannot honestly gate irreversible deletion regardless of how high the ML ceiling looks.

**Why tune the heuristic AND validate ML in parallel:** the 0.813-recall ML ceiling is promising but uninterpretable without hold-out CV (Task 6). If Task 6's hold-out recall also clears 0.85, ML becomes a viable stable alternative to the tuned heuristic; if it falls below 0.85 (likely, given train-set ceiling vs generalization), the tuned heuristic remains the recommended default and ML stays experimental-adjacent. Either way, the threshold tune (Task 5) is the immediate, low-risk fix that clears the floor NOW — it does not depend on the ML outcome.

**Dispatched tasks:** Task 5 (threshold sweep + `HeuristicConfig::default()` tune), Task 6 (k-fold CV subcommand + report). Task 7 is conditional on Task 6 mean recall ≥ 0.85. Task 8 is deferred.

## Post-tuning recall (Tasks 5-7 results)

### Task 5 — heuristic threshold tune

Sweep over [0.25, 0.55] in 0.05 steps found a knee at 0.40 (thresholds
0.25–0.40 are indistinguishable on this gold set — no sentences fall in the
(0.25, 0.40] band that pass all other gates; 0.40 chosen as the most
conservative tied value, closest to the prior 0.5). `HeuristicConfig::default()`
changed `katakana_ratio_threshold: 0.5 → 0.40`.

| metric | before (0.5) | after (0.40) |
|---|---|---|
| recall | 0.8263 | **0.9390** (+0.1127) |
| precision | 0.9514 | 0.9302 (−0.0212) |
| F1 | 0.8844 | **0.9346** (+0.0502) |

Recall now **clears the 0.85 floor**. The 13 remaining false-negatives are the
proper-noun-guard / fragment-over-acceptance edge cases (a Phase 3 concern,
not a threshold-tuning concern).

### Task 6 — ML k-fold cross-validation

5-fold CV (strided assignment, deterministic) on the 300 LLM labels.
Report: `reports/ortho-detect/2026-07-05-phase2.5-ml-cv.md`.

| metric | mean | min | max |
|---|---|---|---|
| recall | **0.9590** | 0.9111 | 1.0000 |
| precision | 0.9673 | 0.9512 | 1.0000 |
| F1 | 0.9629 | 0.9425 | 1.0000 |
| accuracy | 0.9467 | 0.9167 | 1.0000 |

**Task 7 gate (mean recall ≥ 0.85): PASS.** The hold-out mean recall (0.9590)
is essentially equal to the train-accuracy ceiling (0.958) — the ~0 pp gap is
expected because the LLM labels are themselves a learnable linear cascade
(labeler applied the rubric deterministically from character features). This
measures "can the model reproduce LLM labels," NOT "can it detect ortho style
on unseen authors/eras" — that property is unverified (a future-phase
human-gold-set task).

### Task 7 — promote `--ortho-detect ml` from EXPERIMENTAL to stable

ML detector promoted from EXPERIMENTAL to **stable (NOT default)**. CLI help
in `crates/ab-morph-run/src/main.rs` updated: heuristic arm reflects the tuned
threshold (0.40, recall 0.939); ml arm notes 5-fold CV mean recall 0.959 and
keeps the LLM-labels caveat (single-annotator, not human ground truth) +
training-provenance warning.

### Task 8 — code deletion: DEFERRED (does not fire)

Per the Phase 2 ADR, `OrthoTokenizer` / `ortho_compat.rs` / dead OOV config
deletion requires (a) a ≥200-sentence HUMAN-annotated gold set, (b) ML recall
≥ 0.85 against it, (c) ML promoted to default. Phase 2.5 produced an LLM-
labeled evaluation set, NOT human ground truth — so (a) is unmet and deletion
is deferred to a future phase. No code is deleted in Phase 2.5.

## Recommended default mode (Phase 2.5 end-state)

`--ortho-detect heuristic` (the tuned default, threshold 0.40, recall 0.909).
`--ortho-detect ml` is a viable stable alternative (5-fold CV recall 0.947)
for users who want the ML path.

## Human-annotated evaluation set (2026-07-07)

The 300-sentence corpus-representative sample was re-labeled by a human
reviewer using a **three-way** rubric that separates two orthogonal concerns:
(1) is the katakana usage historical orthography? (2) would converting
katakana→hiragana improve tokenization?

| Label | Count | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `accept` | 197 | Historical kanji-katakana-majiri prose — normalize AND record as historical-ortho fact |
| `normalize` | 22 | Emphatic/stylistic/robot-speech katakana — normalize for tokenization, but NOT historical orthography |
| `reject` | 81 | No normalization benefit — onomatopoeia, foreign proper nouns, fragments |

Positive class for recall measurement = `accept` + `normalize` (219 records).

### A.2 Short dialogue fragments at the length boundary (3 sentences)

These are grammatical dialogue fragments 11–12 characters long. They are
above the `short_sentence_max_chars` gate (10) but below the
`long_sentence_min_chars` gate (100), so the `unique_char_ratio` and
repetition-pattern gates fire. The LLM labeler accepts them as grammatical
dialogue; the heuristic's character-level cascade rejects them.

| # | Ratio | Chars | Work | Sentence |
|---|------:|------:|------|----------|
| 9 | 0.45 | 11 | Edogawa_R_Mahouhakase | 「ワハハハハ……。 |
| 11 | 0.58 | 12 | Kobayashi_T_Koujousai | ――ダイジョウブカ。 |
| 12 | 0.55 | 11 | Kobayashi_T_Koujousai | ――ヘコタレルナ。 |

FN 9 is a borderline case — the 「ワハハハハ…… starts with laughter
(typically reject) but contains a grammatical copula fragment that the LLM
labeler treated as accept. FN 11 and 12 are short imperative/interrogative
fragments with dash leaders (――) that the heuristic's repetition/bigram
gates may flag.

### Phase 3 consideration

All 13 FNs are proper-noun-guard or fragment-boundary edge cases, not
threshold-tuning concerns. A future Phase 3 could address these by:
- Removing or relaxing the proper-noun guard (the OOV guard is already
  dead in Branch B; the proper-noun guard is the only remaining
  `OrthoTokenizer` consumer).
- Raising `long_sentence_min_chars` or lowering `short_sentence_max_chars`
  to narrow the "middle-length" band where the uniqueness/repetition gates
  fire.
- Adding a grammatical-structure signal (e.g., sentence contains a verb
  ending like タ/ル/ナイ) to override the proper-noun guard.

Any Phase 3 change must be re-measured against the human gold set (not the
LLM labels) to avoid tuning to LLM-labeler noise.

### Phase 3 consideration

These 14 FPs are NOT a precision crisis (precision only drops from 0.951 to
0.930 after the threshold tune — still strong). They reflect the inherent
blindness of a character-level cascade to semantic distinctions
(onomatopoeia vs prose, proper noun vs grammatical particle). A Phase 3 fix
would require either a semantic classifier or a deliberate precision-recall
tradeoff decision (tighten the gates further, accepting lower recall in
exchange for fewer FPs). Not recommended without a human gold set to
calibrate against.
